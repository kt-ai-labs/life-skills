# Supply History

Maintain `<workspace>/de-stromwechsel/history.md` as a concise history derived from the user's electricity documents. Create it during Phase 0.1 and update it when later user-initiated work reviews relevant contracts or bills. Write it in the language of the first substantive request in the run. Use document evidence for its dates and values; do not reconstruct it from chat memory.

## Layout

Start with a short description of the file's purpose and any material limits in the documents reviewed. Follow it with a table containing these columns, using headings in the report language:

| Supply / billing period (from–to) | Supplier | Billed consumption (kWh) | Total electricity cost for the period | References |
| --------------------------------- | -------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------- | ---------- |

- Use one row per documented annual supply/contract cycle or final shorter period. Reconcile the contract/`Vertragsbestätigung`, supply dates, and bills before choosing rows; an invoice title alone does not define a separate contract or annual cycle. A supplier retained for several years still has separate annual rows.
- When consecutive, non-overlapping invoices partition the same documented annual/contract cycle, combine them into that cycle's single row and sum their billed kWh and total electricity charges. Check compatible supply/meter identity and continuity of dates/readings. Do not merge different annual cycles merely because the supplier matches, and do not sum a bill with a correction, replacement, or cumulative bill that already includes it. Use the applicable corrected amounts once. If coverage or reconciliation is uncertain, leave the affected aggregate unavailable or explicitly limited rather than invent a total.
- Use the covered dates, not invoice issue dates. In a combined row's reference section, identify each contributing invoice's covered period and link it, together with the key confirmation/contract. The table shows only the combined values; no arithmetic explanation is required there. Where a supply period is documented but bills are missing, show the period with billing values unavailable rather than inventing annual boundaries.
- Put the current supply period first and mark it `(current)` in the report language. Show its documented start and any known end date; distinguish a contractual minimum end date from an actual end of supply. If no end is established, show it as ongoing. Until a bill exists, leave its billed consumption and total cost unavailable rather than substituting an estimate, order-form consumption, or projected tariff cost.
- Sort all rows from newest to oldest. Beneath the table, create a section for each row in exactly the same order, with a heading identifying the period and supplier. Give each section a stable, distinct anchor and link to it from the table's References cell.
- In each section, briefly identify the period and link to the important supporting documents, with page or section references where available: contract, `Vertragsbestätigung`, all contributing interim/annual/final bills, and relevant `Preiserhöhung` notices. Use relative links to the files in their original locations. Include only relevant key documents, rather than copying the inventory into history.

## Values and updates

Take billed consumption and total electricity cost from the applicable bill or reconciled invoice slices for the selected period, following the aggregation rules above. The money column is the total charge for electricity supplied over that period, including applicable fixed charges and taxes, before subtracting previously paid Abschläge. Use the document's overall electricity cost (`Gesamtkosten` or equivalent), not its remaining balance, `Guthaben`, refund, `Nachzahlung`, monthly advance, or the amount actually debited from a bank account. Leave unavailable values marked as unavailable; never substitute a settlement balance for total cost.

For the period-level consumption label, inspect the closing meter reading in the latest applicable bill or correction. If it is estimated or derived rather than actually read, label the entire period's kWh as estimate-based, not merely “partially estimated.” An intermediate estimate alone does not make the total estimated if actual opening and closing readings and reconciled meter continuity establish the full-period consumption. If those endpoints or reconciliation remain uncertain, disclose that limitation. A later corrected bill can change this classification. Keep the documented billed electricity charge as billed even when its underlying consumption is estimate-based; do not label it a verified actual cost or confuse it with payment received.

History does not track separate debits, refunds, payment confirmations, or paid/unpaid status. Follow-up actions belong in the task register. Keep source conflicts or corrections traceable in the relevant period section; use the applicable corrected bill and preserve links explaining a changed value. Update existing periods instead of duplicating them, and preserve older documented periods when adding new ones.

The Phase 0.1 task horizon limits task creation, not the periods represented in history. Establish older periods from available bills and key contract records as needed, within the archive access and extraction-effort boundaries. Mark gaps or unreviewed periods clearly; do not imply that an unexamined archive was fully reconstructed. Link history from the current report and internal state instead of duplicating its table there.
