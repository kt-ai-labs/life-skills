# Timestamp policy — material events and temporal provenance

## Timestamp capture

For material events occurring during current work (including recovery and review transitions), actively obtain and capture UTC at the event boundary, using a reliable host/tool event timestamp or an available clock (for example `date -u '+%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%SZ'` where supported). For Executor-controlled transitions capture the time contemporaneously with the actual transition; for external operations prefer their authoritative event timestamp, otherwise capture the time the start/receipt/result is actually observed and label it as such, rather than claiming the unobserved remote occurrence time. Do not defer capture until retrospective bookkeeping. Where a material human or host event precedes authorized record creation, retain the available event/observation time without unauthorized writes; persist only when authorized. Preserve captured times through pending state and recovery. Persist promptly with the corresponding event; reuse the same verified timestamp for genuinely coincident transitions and across records describing the same event. Do not timestamp routine tool calls or repeatedly query the clock while waiting; capture material events when they occur or become known, without delaying human restrictions, creating extra gates or interrupting genuine waiting.

Use partial precision or `unknown` only for a time that genuinely cannot be established from available reliable evidence, such as a historical/recovered event or unavailable time source; state the reason briefly. Do not use `unknown` as a substitute for obtaining an available contemporaneous timestamp. If remote occurrence time is unavailable but receipt/observation is known, record the known time explicitly as the observed event and label the remote occurrence time unknown if relevant. A receipt does not establish a remote start or completion unless its content does. For recovered events distinguish original occurrence, earlier observation (if known) and current recovery/recording; capture current material recovery events under this same policy. If later recording time is material, label it separately, never as the occurrence. Never invent seconds, relabel a later clock reading as the earlier occurrence, or substitute commit/push time for an unrelated event. Missing historical timestamps do not justify rewriting accepted history; append supported corrections under Events and identity. No exhaustive historical timestamp search or repeated probes solely to fill an unknown. If a current capture was missed despite available reliable evidence, record the supported time or honest uncertainty and the omission; correct the capture behavior rather than fabricate provenance. In existing tracking/acceptance checks, identify demonstrated avoidable omissions within the submitted scope and governing revision, using available evidence and the normal severity/correction rules. A justified unknown or partial historical time is not itself a finding; do not retroactively impose a newer capture requirement or require additional instrumentation to prove every clock read.
